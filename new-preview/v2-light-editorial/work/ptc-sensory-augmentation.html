Sensory Augmentation for Robot Operators

Everything a robot operator normally has to guess → spatial cues they can see.

The impossible ask

Yuanzhi Cao had built a bimanual teleoperation system: one hand sets the arm's position and rotation target, the other drives the AGV it rides on. My brief as Reality Lab Intern, under Cao and lab head Valentin Heun, was open: invent new ways to visualise the robot's information and sensor state to make teleoperation easier.

What was invisible

Where the arm is actually pointing, whether its target is in reach, and which part of the arm is about to hit something. An operator normally has to guess all three.

What I built

Simulated sensor feeds in Unity → world-in-miniature + reach line + proximity warnings on HoloLens 2

Simulated sensor and data feeds in Unity (there were no live data feeds) and three spatial cues on top of them: (1) a world-in-miniature view, a small top-down bird's-eye miniature hologram near the operator's hand showing everything around the AGV, which removes the perspective mismatch of teleoperation; (2) a pointing and reach line, from the arm to exactly what it is pointing at, with a large target drawn on that object and colour coding for whether the target is currently within reach; and (3) proximity collision warnings, using simulated distance sensing on the arm, where a growing circle and red highlight appear on the object about to be hit and grow as it gets closer. I also built a test course (pick objects, poke specific buttons, avoid collisions) to try the cues in.

HoloLens 2, Unity, MRTK; simulated Universal Robots arm on an AGV (mobile manipulator), simulated, not live hardware feeds

What it made possible

Knowledge produced, stated honestly. The final presentation reaction was very positive, and the work was seen by R&D leadership including the CTO. The cues were designed to reduce collisions and operator cognitive load, and were informally validated in the self-built test course. There was no user study and no measured collision data, so no measured result is claimed. The LiDAR-style view was simulated, never a live feed. This is also the clearest early evidence of the thread that runs through the later work: making the invisible visible, two years before Startup Central.
