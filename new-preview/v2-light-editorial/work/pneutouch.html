PneuTouch

Virtual objects you cannot feel → a grab you can feel on your wrist.

The impossible ask

Make grabbing a virtual object something your hand can actually feel, without a bulky exoskeleton or a tethered glove.

What was invisible

Contact itself: in VR you can see your hand close on an object, but nothing tells your body that the grab happened, or how close it is to happening.

What I built

Unity proximity layers → ESP32 over Wi-Fi → servo primes, then pump fires the grab

Working for Robert LiKamWa, I built the whole Unity to device loop. Layered Unity colliders carry the interaction logic: crossing a proximity layer moves the servo to a primed position (near the hand, not yet grabbing), and crossing the closer layer fires the grab. I wrote the ESP32 servo and pump firmware that reads those triggers over Wi-Fi at the right moments, and I helped build the device itself: electrical components, pumps and tubes, and wrist fit and component placement alongside its industrial designer.

Unity / C#, ESP32 over Wi-Fi, servo and pump pneumatics, layered-collider interaction design

What it made possible

Knowledge produced. Co-author on the PneuTouch paper at the CHI '22 Workshop on Sustainable Haptics. The workshop paper presents the prototype and reports no user study, so no study results are claimed here. The case is also the core evidence for my Unity to embedded bridge work: one system spanning interaction design, firmware, and physical hardware.
