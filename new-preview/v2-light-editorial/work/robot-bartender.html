Robot Bartender

Rehearse a robot's every move in your headset → watch the real machine pour.

The impossible ask

Program a mobile dual-arm robot bartender, safely, quickly, and without learning the hard way on real hardware.

What was invisible

IK failures, reach limits, and collisions: everything that would go wrong when two 6-axis UR20 arms on a 3-axis platform move through a real kitchen, invisible until the metal moves.

What I built

Vision Pro planning tool → Unity ↔ Python server ↔ two UR PolyScope simulators → real arms

Software and programming only (no mechanical or electrical build claimed). Plan movements, dances, and drink service in-headset against a full 3D model of the robot and kitchen for collision preview. The strongest piece is the palletising system: author one grip A-B-C sequence for a single glass or can, auto-apply offsets across the whole grid, and pre-simulate every resulting IK path to catch reach and singularity errors before running on hardware. The planning tool was re-tooled from my earlier Vision Pro robot-programming app: one tool, two robots.

Unity / Vision Pro planning tool, Unity ↔ Python server ↔ two UR PolyScope simulator instances, IK, palletising logic

What it made possible

Delivery and adoption and use. Complete and in use. Installed in the client's home: order on an iPad, the robot takes glasses from a grid and serves draft beer (also ice water, and dances) at the owner's parties. Beyond that one client it went nowhere commercially, stated plainly, with no dollar figure attached.
