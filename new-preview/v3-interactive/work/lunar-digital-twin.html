ASU Digital Discovery Initiative · Aug 2024 to Sep 2025

Lunar Digital Twin

Terrain, slope, and power you can't feel from a spreadsheet → a Moon you can drive before anyone has to go there.

The impossible ask

Let mission planners rehearse driving a real rover over real lunar terrain, and judge routes, slope, and power before anyone commits to a path on the Moon.

What was invisible

Orbital imagery and elevation data are hard to reason about in tables and flat maps: how steep a slope actually feels, where a route pinches, and what a drive would cost in power.

What I built

LROC imagery + DEMs → Python / GDAL pipelines → Unity VR + desktop mission-planning tool

As Lead VR Developer I hired, trained, and managed seven student developers, and was the lead technical authority reporting to director Kip Hodges. We built a multi-user VR and desktop tool that drives Lunar Outpost's actual Eagle rover model over real lunar terrain (VIPER areas, Artemis candidate sites, and an Apollo 17 recreation), with A-B-C route planning, points-of-interest annotation, and evaluation of slope, elevation cross-sections, and power usage. Underneath it, I built the terrain pipelines that turn source imagery and elevation data into usable 3D terrain.

Unity / C#, Unity Netcode for GameObjects, desktop companion, Python / GDAL, Cesium 3D Tiles, LROC imagery and DEMs, NASA Ames Stereo Pipeline, Blender. Lunar source data is about 0.5 to 1 m per pixel, and accuracy is stated per domain on purpose.

What it made possible

Decision enabled and capability built. Lunar Outpost showed the team's top-down rover and mission-site video and terrain images to NASA during LTV selection presentations. The pipeline numbers are the hard metrics: image stitching went from 18 minutes to 1 minute 36 seconds, and image plus DEM to 3D mesh went from days of manual work to about 4 minutes. Both pipelines are still in use after my departure, under a successor I trained. No completed user study is claimed for this work; the approach was co-authored in a presentation at AGU Fall Meeting 2024.
